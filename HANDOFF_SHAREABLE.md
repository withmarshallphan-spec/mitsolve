# HANDOFF: MIT Solve 2027 application (shareable version)

This version has no personal or location details. Where local facts mattered, they're described in general terms. The partner who wrote this keeps the detailed local research and will share specific facts as needed.

> **To the AI reading this:**

> - Don't repeat the dead ends below unless you have new evidence.

## 0. Hard facts

- **Deadline: Friday 6 November 2026, 12:00 pm EST (17:00 UTC).** Confirmed on Solve's official challenge pages on 4 Nov. Late submissions are refused for any reason. Aim to submit well before then.
- **Stage rule:** Solve requires a *functioning initial prototype*, not a mock-up. It may have zero users yet. Concept-stage ideas are only accepted in the separate Indigenous Communities Fellowship.
- **Format:** English only. Most answers are about 200 words; innovation and business model about 300. Solve warns that **generic or AI-sounding text seriously hurts** applications. Write in a human voice.
- **2027 challenges:** Health, Climate, Learning and Economic Prosperity. The Learning challenge explicitly wants **gains in what learners can actually do**, not engagement. Its dimensions include assessment that enables intervention, low-resource delivery and teacher support.
- **Judging criteria:** alignment, potential impact, feasibility, innovation, human-centred design, scalability, partnership potential.
- **Selectivity:** in 2025, 31 teams were selected from 2,900+ applications (about 1%).
- **Team:** two partners, each with their own local network. Each can only test with people they can actually reach.
- **Goal:** submit something real and honest. Showing up matters more than winning this cycle.

## 1. Tests every idea must pass

1. **A real, serious problem in society**, not an inconvenience and not a "helper".
2. **It removes the actual cause.**
   - The benchmark is **OkHi**, a 2025 Solve winner: a definite problem (homes with no digital address) and a definite solution (they get a valid digital address).
   - Ask: does our solution remove the blocker, or only help people struggle past it?
3. **Something new**, not a feature of an existing product or a government programme.
4. **The outcome is visible:** did it work, yes or no?
5. **Real validation is possible by the deadline,** with real reachable people and no institutional approval or private data needed.
6. **Not already done** by a past Solve winner or a dominant incumbent.
7. **The people who need it can access it:** phones, literacy, time, motivation.

## 2. What was tried and why it failed

### Idea 1: Medicine before the disruption (Health). Dead.
- **What it was:** forecasts and clinic-closure notices tell a clinic which chronic patients (HIV, TB, insulin, blood pressure) will run out of medicine before access returns. It then plans early refills, deliveries, stock transfers and messages that don't name the condition. A full offline web app was built and tested.
- **Why it died:**
  - **Wrong blocker.** In 2025 funding cuts, patients fell out of care because staff, medicine, tracing and records disappeared. In a 38-country survey, tracing was disrupted in 67% of clinics. Nobody was missing a list of who to refill.
  - **A feature, not a product.** Clinic record systems already produce "due for refill" lists.
  - **False premise.** Funding cuts took effect overnight, so there was no warning to plan around.
  - **Can't be validated by a student.** It needs clinics, patient data and approvals.

### Idea 2: Job-offer scam checker (Economic Prosperity). Dead.
- **What it was:** a user forwards a suspicious job message and gets a verdict: is it a verified HR channel, is it an illegal fee request, are there phishing links.
- **Why it died:**
  - **Wrong blocker for the biggest harm.** The largest losses are fees people pay *knowingly* to brokers who sell insider access to jobs. Victims know the broker isn't official, so a checker doesn't stop it. It catches only impersonation and phishing, the smaller losses.
  - **Incumbents.** The national labour ministry is building verified vacancy listings and per-vacancy QR codes. An overseas-agency registry already exists. Fact-checking chatbots already run on WhatsApp.
  - **It needs real data.** A checker only works if a real employer or organisation confirms its genuine contacts. Without that it's guessing.
  - **Evidence was overstated.** A "hundreds of victims" headline turned out to be 140.

### Other candidates screened out
- **Water-shortage information app:** the blocker is physical water.
- **Trafficking routes:** victims can't be reached safely.
- **Illegal-loan checker:** the financial regulator already runs one.
- **Heat alerts, mangrove reporting, skills certificates:** don't remove the blocker.
- **Factory-dormitory mental health:** not safe for students to run.
- **Schooling for undocumented children; evictions:** the blockers are ID papers and land rights.

### Idea 3: Parents check their child's reading and maths level over WhatsApp (Learning). Revised.
- **The objections raised, and what the evidence said:**
  - **"Low scores are because education isn't distributed."** *Mostly false.* Children are in school but not learning. A 2000–2014 study in a large middle-income country found enrolment rose while learning fell in every group. In PISA, even the richer students score low.
  - **"Poor families don't have phones."** *Mostly false at household level:* over 90% of households have a phone, though only about 70% of individuals do. *True in remote rural areas.*
  - **"Parents lack the time, skill or interest."** *True enough to kill the parent-run version.* It would reach the families who need it least.

### Current direction: assess, teach at the child's level, check again (Learning). Alive, not validated.
- **What it is:**
  1. A short level check: letters, words, sentences, story; numbers, subtraction, division.
  2. A **human tutor** teaches at exactly that level, by phone or in person.
  3. A different but comparable task is done without help.
  4. Repeat weekly. The parent only hands over the phone.
- **Why it survived:** it is modelled on **ConnectEd (Youth Impact)**: one 20-minute phone tutoring call a week plus SMS problems. Randomised trials in six countries found gains everywhere, averaging about 0.30–0.35 SD, at about US$12 per child, on basic phones. It also follows **Teaching at the Right Level**, one of the most cost-effective education methods known.
- **Independent check:** a separate AI analysis of the same fact base reached the same direction.
- **Open weaknesses:**
  1. **Novelty.** Similar programmes already exist: ConnectEd, Rocket Learning (India, a 2026 Solve prize winner), Bookbot (a reading app), Pemantik (a teacher toolkit), Wadhwani AI.
     - What's new has to come from the delivery model. The candidate angle is **young people as tutors**, since high-school leavers are a large share of the unemployed in at least one partner's region.
     - Partners in different countries using **one shared tool** could also count.
  2. **Demand is unproven.** Will families agree, and will children stay on the call?
  3. **Tutor capacity and cost** limit how far it can grow.
  4. **Learning gains take weeks.** Before the deadline, only a small before/after task is possible.
- **Go/no-go:** at least 3 consenting families plus a tutor before building further.

## 3. Comparison points
- **OkHi:** clear problem, clear fix.
- **ConnectEd:** proven phone tutoring at the child's level.
- **Rocket Learning:** WhatsApp programme for parents; doesn't track each child's level.
- **Bookbot:** reading-only app; requires an app install.
- **Pemantik:** level toolkit, for teachers.
- Past Solve winners: OkHi, ABALOBI, Karya, Kara Solar, NiADA, Smart Scope CX, Visilant and others.

## 4. Mistakes made (don't repeat)
1. Chose the solution before testing the real cause of the harm.
2. Built a polished prototype before validating the problem.
3. Trusted single-source numbers.
4. Assumed advance warning existed when it didn't.
5. Assumed people (parents) would do work they lack the time or skill for.
6. Kept researching when the missing piece was talking to real users.

## 5. Prototype spec (agreed)
1. Runs on an ordinary phone or laptop, and works offline or printed.
2. **Records** a pseudonymous learner ID (never a name), consent, and the adult's guess of the child's level, taken *before* the result is shown.
3. **Level check:** short and human-scored, in the learner's language, with ASER-style levels. Don't call it a validated diagnostic.
4. **Picks practice:** a transparent rule chooses an activity matched to the level. Content is human-reviewed; no AI needed for this step.
5. **Logs the session:** what was taught and how much help was needed.
6. **Post-check:** a different but comparable task done without help. Raw answers are saved.
7. **Exports** records and shows the next step.
8. **Privacy:** minimal data, no stored child voice recordings, and records can be deleted.
9. **Languages:** support each partner's local language plus English, so each partner can test locally.

Skip WhatsApp Business API automation: the test number reaches only 5 people and needs approval. Use plain calls and messages plus the web tool.

## 6. Plan
1. Confirm the challenge and deadline in the application portal.
2. **Each partner recruits 3+ families locally**: consent, plus the adult's guess of the child's level.
3. Build the section 5 prototype.
4. Run a check, one session and a post-check per child. Record everything truthfully: invitations, refusals, results, problems.
5. Write the application in a human voice: what was built, who tested it and how many, what happened, and what isn't known yet.
6. Submit at least 12 hours before the deadline.
